How it is joined

Pinned through-tenon

Back rail passes through both stiles and is pinned with riven walnut dowel, visible on the outer face.

Blind keyed tenon

Seat rails enter the legs on blind tenons locked by an internal walnut key — invisible, and it will not creep.

Kigoroshi

All chair tenons are compressed before assembly; a chair joint that starts tight and swells will not develop the seasonal click.

Dimensions & alterations

The dimensions above are our standard set-out. Length, width and height can be cut to your room on request — send us the constraint and we will return a dimensioned drawing and a revised price.

Because the joinery is laid out full-size before cutting, a change in dimension is a change in set-out, not an adaptation of a standard part. Nothing is compromised by asking.

Care

Dry or barely damp cloth. No solvents, no silicone polish. Re-oil surfaces in daily use about once a year — we send a bottle of the same hardwax oil with every piece.

Expect a few millimetres of seasonal movement across wide panels. That is the joinery working as designed.

Delivery & assembly

Lead time 8–10 weeks from confirmed drawing. Fifty per cent deposit to start, balance on completion.

Larger pieces ship knocked-down and assemble with a rubber mallet. There are no fixings to lose, no tools required, and no allen key taped inside the packaging.

The thinking

A chair is the hardest thing to build without metal. Every joint is loaded in a different direction each time somebody sits, leans back, or drags it in.

The Tsugi answers with through-tenons where the loads are highest — seat rail to leg, and back rail to stile — each one pinned rather than glued alone. The exposed tenon ends on the back rail are the structure admitting what it is.

It weighs 4.6kg. You can lift it with one finger under the seat.
